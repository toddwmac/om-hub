Three Structural Shifts This Report Tracks

  • Capital concentration: Rounds under $50M fell from ~72% of VC dollars (2010) to ~47% (2015) to ~38% (2024). Mega-rounds now dominate.
  • Sector concentration: AI's 50%+ share of funding exceeds any prior wave — dot-com (~39%), mobile (~31%), crypto (~22%).
  • Selectivity: Fewer, larger deals; a "barbell" market where AI-capable companies raise easily while others face a funding drought.

Ecosystem Effects — Beyond the Funding Numbers

  • The barbell market: Strong, often AI-driven companies attract abundant capital while nearly everyone else struggles — a stark bifurcation of the opportunity set.
  • Mega-round economics: Single rounds (e.g., Anthropic's ~$13B Series F) now exceed what entire national ecosystems once raised annually.
  • Non-AI funding drought: With majority share flowing to one paradigm, non-AI startups compete for a shrinking pool and face longer cycles and higher bars.
  • Corporate gravity: Megacap tech is committing hundreds of billions to AI infrastructure — creating partnership and acquisition openings, but also raising the competitive bar for startups in incumbent-dominated categories.
  • Physical AI & infrastructure: Enthusiasm is spreading beyond software into chips, robotics, and autonomy, with landmark AI-hardware IPOs validating the full stack.
  • Cheaper building, pricier winning: AI tooling lets tiny teams ship faster with less capital — yet winners are expected to consume more capital than ever, pushing mega-rounds further.

How the Story Changed Over the Decade

  • Mid-2010s framing: The classic CB Insights post-mortem study put "no market need" first (~42%), "ran out of cash" second (~29%), and "not the right team" third.
  • Post-2022 reframing: Newer analysis flips the lens — 70% "ran out of capital," but that's the final cause of death; PMF, timing, and unit economics explain why the capital dried up.
  • The ZIRP hangover: Sectors that raised heavily in 2021–22 on trends that never materialized (alt-protein, NFTs, some climate plays) drove a disproportionate share of shutdowns.
  • Unit economics matter again: Unsustainable economics (~19% of failures) reflect the era's shift from growth-at-all-costs to efficiency.
  • Perennials: Ignoring user feedback, tunnel vision, weak go-to-market, mispricing, and founder discord recur across every era's data.

3 · Founder Skills Rising to the Top

What VCs underwrite hasn't changed at the core — but the AI era and the post-2022 correction re-weighted the scorecard. Emerging Renewing

What VCs Say They Value in Founders (% of VCs Citing, Gompers et al.)

AI-Native Fluency Emerging

Deep, working knowledge of AI gives founders enormous leverage. Investors increasingly favor companies architected around AI from day one — not retrofitted.

Capital Efficiency & "Cockroach Mode" Emerging

With a high funding bar and long cycles, founders who hit milestones on lean budgets — and survive between rounds — stand out.

Distribution & Go-To-Market Craft Emerging

As AI collapses the cost of building product, defensibility shifts to distribution, positioning, and speed to revenue — weak GTM is a top documented failure cause.

Data/Evidence-Driven Storytelling Emerging

In a selective market, traction signals — revenue, adoption, retention — must be woven into a compelling, credible narrative.

Execution Ability Renewing

The single most-cited attribute (67% of VCs). Demonstrated capacity to turn ideas into shipped, adopted product is the core underwrite.

Domain Expertise Renewing

Valued by 60% of VCs; experienced domain-specific founders significantly outperform first-timers on long-term survival.

Coachability & Adaptability Renewing

Openness to feedback and willingness to pivot — the inability to adapt strategy to market signals kills even well-funded companies.

Resilience, Grit & Leadership Renewing

Investors now explicitly assess grit, team-building, and culture-setting; leadership is treated as the defining differentiator when technologies look comparable.

The Net-Net for Founders

  • The bet is still on the person: markets shift and products pivot, but founder character and capability determine outcomes.
  • Passion is table stakes (54% of VCs cite it) — obsession plus evidence of execution wins the meeting.
  • Complementary founding teams beat solo generalists: identify your "spikes" and hire around your gaps.
  • In 2025's market: be AI-literate, be lean, show traction, and prove you can lead through storms.
